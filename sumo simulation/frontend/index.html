Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) Mathematical Formulation

Classical ALNS metaheuristic implementation based on Ropke & Pisinger (2006) for Dynamic Multi-Vehicle CVRP.

1. Algorithmic Principles & Operator Suite

Destroy Operators (Adaptive Removal)
  • Random Removal: Uniformly unselects customer nodes at random to broaden exploration.
  • Worst Removal: Computes exact marginal cost contribution and removes expensive stops with Shaw greediness (p=3).
  • Shaw Removal: Clusters related customers using distance and package demand disparity.
Repair Operators (Heuristic Insertion)
  • Random Repair: Re-inserts unassigned customers into random feasible vehicle routes.
  • Greedy Repair: Evaluates all feasible insertion positions across fleet vehicles and greedily selects minimum cost increase.
  • Regret-2 / Regret-3 Repair: Computes regret penalty (second-best minus best insertion) to prioritize constrained stops.
Adaptive Weight Adjustment & Simulated Annealing Acceptance

Weights are adapted every segment of 10 iterations using roulette wheel probabilities with decay rate λ = 0.8. Operators receive reward scores (σ₁ = 33 for new best, σ₂ = 9 for improved incumbent, σ₃ = 13 for accepted Simulated Annealing transition with cooling rate c = 0.97).

Hybrid Genetic Search (HGS) Mathematical Formulation

State-of-the-art population-based memetic metaheuristic based on Vidal et al. (2012, 2014, 2022) with Prins' DP Split, dual subpopulations, and biased fitness.

1. Chromosome Representation & Optimal Partitioning

Giant-Tour Chromosome & Prins' Split

Chromosomes represent a giant tour sequence without route delimiters. Optimal vehicle route partitioning is evaluated via Prins' Dynamic Programming Split algorithm in O(N · B) time, finding the globally optimal fleet allocation for any given sequence.

Dual Subpopulations & Dynamic Penalties

Maintains two parallel subpopulations: Feasible and Infeasible. Infeasible individuals are evaluated with a capacity violation penalty parameter ω_cap, which dynamically adapts every 20 offspring to maintain feasibility within [20%, 50%].

Biased Fitness & Broken-Pairs Diversity

To prevent premature convergence, HGS computes the broken-pairs sequence distance measuring edge disparities. Survivor selection balances solution cost rank with population diversity contribution via Biased Fitness.
